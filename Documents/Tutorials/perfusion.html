ASL perfusion analysis

Acquisition-aware BASIL processing and cerebral blood flow QC

Research training guide · Updated 14 October 2026. Examples use placeholder paths and require adaptation to your acquisition and installed software. Test one participant before scaling to a cohort.

1. Decode the acquisition

Determine whether the acquisition is PASL or pCASL, the label/control ordering, timing scheme, repeats and calibration strategy. Obtain labelling duration, post-label delays or inversion times, slice timing for 2D acquisitions and labelling efficiency assumptions. Keep a record of the M0 calibration image and its relationship to the ASL run.

2. Prepare a verified configuration

Open the BASIL GUI or build an oxford_asl command using documentation for your installed release. The GUI can help construct the command. Start by checking available options:

oxford_asl --help
# Configure input ordering, timing, calibration and registration
# from the acquisition protocol; save the final command in your log.

There is no universal command for all ASL acquisitions. Verify whether the software expects inversion time or post-label delay in the selected mode. Review label/control ordering before interpreting the sign of the difference signal.

3. Process and register

Use a workflow appropriate to the acquisition for motion correction, distortion correction, perfusion modelling, calibration and anatomical registration. With multi-delay data, assess arterial transit time as well as perfusion. Evaluate partial-volume correction deliberately rather than enabling it without checking tissue segmentation and alignment.

4. Review outputs

  • Inspect raw pairs and difference images for motion or subtraction artefacts.
  • Check ASL-to-anatomical alignment, especially near frontal and temporal regions.
  • Review calibration-image quality and model fit.
  • Check vascular signal, coverage, negative or extreme values and between-session consistency.

Document whether values are calibrated cerebral blood flow or relative perfusion. Calibrated outputs require validated calibration and unit conventions. Preserve maps, registration evidence, processing settings and regional coverage with every exported table.

Record your analysis

Retain the participant/session manifest, input checksums, software versions, commands, completion status, QC reviewer and decision, and reasons for missing or excluded measurements. Keep raw inputs separate from derivatives.
